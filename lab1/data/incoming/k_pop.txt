K-pop, short for Korean popular music, is a genre of music originating in South Korea that is heavily influenced by a variety of styles and genres from around the world, including pop, experimental, rock, jazz, gospel, hip hop, R&B, reggae, electronic dance, folk, and traditional Korean music on top of its traditional roots. Modern K-pop idol culture emerged with the formation of the landmark South Korean boy group Seo Taiji and Boys in 1992, whose experimentation with different music styles and genres helped reshape and modernize South Korea's contemporary music scene. Today, K-pop has evolved into a global phenomenon characterized by highly polished audiovisual productions, synchronized choreography, dedicated fandoms, and a massive international reach spanning digital platforms and stadium tours across continents.
